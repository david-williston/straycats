## The idea

"Stray" and "pet" aren't two separate boxes. A cat might be one family's pet, fed by three
other households, and called a stray by the restaurant on the corner. Some cats really are
lost, and their owners are searching for them.

If ownership is just another **claim**, not a fact, then:

- a family could say "this is our Milo", alongside the neighbour's claim that it's "Ninja"
- an owner looking for a lost cat could be matched against recent encounters nearby
- everyone could see that a "stray" actually has people looking out for it

## Many homes, many claims

It happens at the house all the time. [Tenkilo](/ideas/meeting-a-cat/), named for weighing
almost ten kilos, is clearly being fed at a comfortable home nearby, and so is Mika Ella, who
barely nibbles when she's back here. Ciccio isn't welcome there, and eats only here. Does that
make Tenkilo their cat? They may think so.

People can be possessive, too. Les used to feed a neighbour's outdoor cat salmon, until the
neighbour asked Les to stop: the cat was "on a special diet". A cat can have several homes, and
the people in each may not know about, or approve of, the others.

## Meeting isn't claiming

Meeting a cat, naming it, even feeding it, doesn't make it yours, and Stray Cats should never
suggest it does. Your list of cats you've met is a list of acquaintances, not possessions.

That list can still help. When someone is searching for a lost cat, people who've met a
similar cat nearby recently could be asked: *"Have you seen this cat?"* The answer might be
in someone's photo from yesterday. (More in [When a Cat Goes Missing](/ideas/when-a-cat-goes-missing/).)

## Open questions

- How do you handle conflicting ownership claims without starting neighbourhood feuds?
- Should we connect to existing lost-pet services rather than duplicate them?
- How do you stop people falsely claiming an animal?
- How do you show that a cat eats in several homes without causing friction between them?
