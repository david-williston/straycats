## The idea

A lot of what goes wrong for street cats isn't cruelty. It's not knowing: about poison,
about sterilisation, about black cats, about what happens when feeding stops. Stray Cats
could help people learn these things a little at a time, at the moment it matters, without
lecturing anyone.

## What people could learn

**Sterilisation changes everything.** Someone adopts a kitten, doesn't get it spayed, and a
year later there's a litter, and then a household with five cats and not enough money to
feed them. Here, sterilising a cat costs around 900 pesos (about CA$80): affordable for some,
out of reach for many. Knowing where low-cost sterilisation is available, and when a cat is
old enough, could prevent a lot of hunger.

**Poison kills cats, by accident and on purpose.** Rat bait is a common accidental killer:
a cat eats a poisoned rat and is poisoned too, even though cats were the natural rat
control in the first place (thanks to Les for that point). Some people also poison cats
deliberately. Knowing safer ways to deal with rats, and recognising the signs of poisoning,
could save lives. The deliberate kind is also why Stray Cats will never show where a cat
lives (see [Privacy & Animal Safety](/ideas/privacy-and-safety/)).

**Black cats deserve better.** Black cats are often said to be adopted less and put down
more in shelters, partly out of superstition. In my experience they're some of the sweetest
cats there are: once [Ciccio](/ideas/meeting-a-cat/) trusted me, she simply melted.

**Feeding is a commitment.** When feeding stops, even for a few days because a caretaker is
ill, cats quickly start hunting and fighting. Cats also catch wildlife: Les's tortoiseshell
once brought in a hummingbird. Regular feeding, and someone to cover when a caretaker can't,
matters for the cats and for the birds.

## How Stray Cats could help

- **A tip at the right moment.** Meet a cat whose ear isn't tipped, and learn what
  sterilisation involves and where to find it nearby.
- **Local knowledge, shared.** Low-cost sterilisation campaigns, vets who help community
  cats, safer rat control.
- **Stories over rules.** Ninja's recovery and Ciccio's sweetness may change more minds than
  any list of facts.

## Open questions

- How do you share information without lecturing, or making anyone feel judged for what they
  can't afford?
- Who keeps local information (campaigns, vets, prices) accurate and up to date?
- How do you talk about deliberate poisoning without frightening people, or giving anyone
  ideas?
- Should Stray Cats help people report poisoning, and to whom?
