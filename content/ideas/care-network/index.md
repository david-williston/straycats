*Background: [The cats nobody owns](/posts/the-cats-nobody-owns/)*

## The idea

Around a single cat there may be several people: one feeds it in the morning, another in
the evening, a restaurant leaves out scraps, and someone once paid for its deworming. None
of them know about each other.

If they could see that they're all caring for the same cat, they could:

- avoid double-feeding, and avoid double-treating with medication
- share vet and sterilisation costs
- notice sooner when a cat goes missing or gets sick
- coordinate TNR (trap–neuter–return) with local groups
- hand over care when someone leaves town, as I eventually will

## From meeting to caring

Most people will only ever meet a cat. Some will see it again and again. A few will feed it,
take it to the vet, or arrange its sterilisation. All of them matter:

- **Someone who's met a cat once** can still flag something: it looks thin, it's limping,
  it hasn't been seen for weeks.
- **Regulars** notice changes first, because they know what normal looks like.
- **Caretakers** can see those flags and each other, and act.

Meeting a cat should make it easy to take the next step, if someone wants to: "I fed it
today", "I can help with the vet bill". Never pushed, always possible.

## Open questions

- How do caretakers connect without exposing personal details?
- Should shared costs be tracked, or is that a step too far?
- What do rescue and TNR groups need that individuals don't?
- How do you turn "this cat looks sick" from someone passing by into help, quickly?
