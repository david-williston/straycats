## The concern

A public map of exactly where stray cats live and get fed is useful to helpers — but also
to people who want to harm animals, or who object to colonies in their area. Caretakers'
own locations and routines also need protecting.

## Possible approaches

- **Fuzzed public locations** — show an approximate area, not an exact point.
- **Trusted roles** — verified rescuers/caretakers see precise data.
- **Delayed publishing** — sightings appear publicly after a delay.
- **Minimal personal data** — collect as little about users as possible.

## Celebrate cats without exposing them

People love the cats in their neighbourhood, and Stray Cats should let them enjoy and share
them. But some things that make an app exciting would put cats at risk, so they're ruled out:

- **No popularity.** No rankings, no "most-met" or "favourite" cat, no counts that make a cat
  famous. A well-known cat is easier to find, for good people and bad.
- **No exact spots, anywhere.** Not on cards, not in shared links, not in landmarks or place
  names that give a feeding spot away. Your own private map can remember where *you* met a
  cat; nobody else sees it.
- **No public photo links.** Photos are shown inside cards, not as files anyone can pass
  around and reuse.
- **No people in photos,** and nothing that shows someone's home.
- **Welfare over engagement.** Features are judged by whether they help cats, not by how much
  they're used.

## Open questions

- Where's the right balance between openness and safety?
- Who decides who is "trusted"?
- How do you let people share a cat they love without sharing where it lives?
