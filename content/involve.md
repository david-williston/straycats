There's no app to download and nothing to sign up for. Stray Cats is a notebook where I'm
thinking out loud, and the most useful thing you can do is think alongside me. Below are some
ways to do that, from a single line to a proper conversation. Start wherever feels easy. None
of it adds up to a commitment, for you or for me.

**Every message is read by me, personally, and I'll write back.** English or Spanish, either
is fine.

{{< contact-channels >}}

## Say hi, in one line

The smallest useful message is a name and a place: "I'm in Oaxaca and there's a one-eyed cat
at my corner shop." That's a whole message. Use Telegram, WhatsApp or email, whichever you
already have open.

## Answer one question

If you'd like something to respond to, pick one and answer in a sentence or two. Disagreement
is welcome. It's how the idea gets better.

- Do you look after cats you don't technically own? How do you keep track of them?
- Have you met a cat with more than one name, or more than one "owner"?
- What happens to the cats when caretakers move away?
- Which [ideas](/ideas/) excite you? Which ones worry you?
- What would make you stop and record a cat you meet?
- What should a cat's card never show?

Every story and idea also ends with a **Respond to this idea** box, with a question about
that page.

## Share a cat story, an idea or a correction

If you'd rather it be written down where others can see it, [open an
issue](https://github.com/david-williston/straycats/issues/new/choose) on GitHub. It's the
right place for a cat's story, a new idea, or something I got wrong. For open conversation
there's also [GitHub Discussions](https://github.com/david-williston/straycats/discussions).
A free GitHub account is all you need, and you don't have to be a developer. Every page also
has a **Suggest changes** link.

Please leave out exact locations and home addresses. A cat's neighbourhood is plenty (see
[Privacy & Animal Safety](/ideas/privacy-and-safety/)).

## Offer a skill

Translation, design, development, rescue or vet experience, local knowledge: the vet who
helps community cats in your town, where low-cost sterilisation happens, how things really
work where you live. There's no code yet, [on purpose](/posts/all-talk-no-code/), so there
isn't a team to join. But if you tell me what you're good at, I'll know who to ask when
there's something real to work on, and you're free to say no then.

## Have a chat

If you'd rather talk than type, book a relaxed 30-minute video chat about the cats in your
life and your ideas. It's the biggest thing on this list, and entirely optional. Chats are
in English, cameras are optional, and the [Let's talk](/talk/) page explains what happens
to what you say.
