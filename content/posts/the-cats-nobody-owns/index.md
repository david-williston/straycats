I'm living for a while in Puerto Escondido, on the Pacific coast of Mexico, in a house with
a lot of cats. Some are pets. Some are strays. Some are of unknown origin. The owner of the
house is in Italy, so while I'm here, I look after them.

{{< photo src="the-path.jpg" alt="Ninja, a pale orange-and-white cat, in front; Ciccio, a black cat, behind; and Mika Ella with a black-and-white cat further up the garden path" caption="Ninja, Ciccio and Mika Ella, with a black-and-white friend. Pets, strays, or somewhere in between?" >}}

I buy their food out of my own pocket. Sometimes I pay for a vet visit, or deworming
medication. Nobody asked me to — it's just what you do when a cat is sitting at your door
looking at you.

I'm sure I'm not the only person in the world doing this. In fact I think there are a lot
of us: people quietly becoming **informal custodians of cats we don't technically own**.
We feed them, we recognise individuals, we notice when one disappears or gets sick, we pay
for sterilisation or vet care — usually with no coordination with the other people doing
exactly the same thing a few streets away.

## The question

Most systems for animals assume every cat needs an *owner*. But what if the more useful
starting point is simply this:

> **A cat with an identity, a history, and a network of people who care about it.**

Identify the cat. Create its record. Let every human encounter after that add to its shared
history.

That feels like the seed of something. I don't yet know what it grows into — this blog is
where I'm going to find out, in public, with your help.

(I'm focusing on cats for now. Dogs are welcome eventually — they're just a different type
of cat.)
