When someone new arrives at the house, I introduce them to the cats. This is Ciccio. That's
Tenkilo, in the sun as usual. The orange-and-white one by the bowl? We call it Mountain
Lion; nobody knows its real name.

And when people meet a cat, they take a photo. Almost everyone does. It's the most natural
thing in the world.

Then the photo disappears into a camera roll, somewhere between a receipt and a sunset. The
name I told them fades. The cat goes back to being "a black cat I saw in Mexico".

## What if the photo was the introduction?

Imagine taking that photo and, a few seconds later, getting the cat's card:

{{< cat-cards names="Ciccio, Tenkilo, Mountain Lion" >}}

A name, a coat, one thing worth knowing. If the cat is already known, you meet it properly:
here's what other people know about it. If it isn't, you're the first, and your photo is the
start of its story.

That would be a lovely thing for the person holding the phone. But the real point is the cat.
Every one of those photos is an [encounter](/ideas/encounters/): one more piece of the cat's
history, one more person who recognises it, maybe one more person who notices when it's thin,
or limping, or gone.

## Fun is the doorway, not the destination

I want meeting a cat to feel like a small gift, because that's how people will keep doing it.
But it's easy to imagine what happens if "fun" takes over: the most photogenic cats get the
attention, and attention isn't always good for a cat that lives on the street. A cat's exact spot becomes
something anyone can find. A cat becomes an attraction.

So the rule I keep coming back to is simple: **if a fun feature and the cat's wellbeing ever
disagree, the cat wins.** No rankings. No exact spots. No photos floating around without the
cat's story attached.

I've written up the details in a new idea page, [Meeting a Cat](/ideas/meeting-a-cat/). What
would make *you* stop and record a cat you meet? And what should a cat's card never show?
