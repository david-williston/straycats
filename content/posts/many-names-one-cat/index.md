Some of the cats that visit me are obviously being fed very well somewhere else. I don't
know where. I don't know whether those people think of the cat as their pet, or as a stray
they happen to feed. And I don't know what *they* call it — so I've given it my own name.
Which means these cats now have several names.

Imagine one physical cat:

- To me, it's **Ninja**.
- Three streets away, someone calls it **Naranja**.
- To the restaurant on the corner, it's an unnamed stray.
- To a family nearby, it's **Milo**, their beloved outdoor pet.

All of those perspectives can be true at the same time.

## What this tells us

**A cat's identity can't be its name, its location, or its owner** — because all three are
ambiguous. Underneath, there has to be a single *cat*, with many human claims attached:

- *I call this cat Ninja.*
- *I see it here, most mornings.*
- *I feed it.*
- *I think it's a stray.*
- *I think it belongs to someone.*
- *I last saw it on Tuesday.*

And the important bit: the system **shouldn't force everyone to agree too early**. It
should gather evidence, and over time recognise that what looked like separate records are
probably the **same cat**.

That changes the idea from a cat directory into something more interesting: **a way to
piece together the fragmented truth about an animal from many independent human
encounters.**

Do you know a cat with more than one name? I'd love to hear about it.
